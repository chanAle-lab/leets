# Topic 5.3 · Material Properties & Mechanical Testing

## Mechanical Properties Definitions

- **Tensile Strength (UTS):** Maximum tensile stress a material withstands before necking and fracture occur ($\\sigma_u = F_{max}/A_0$).
- **Hardness:** Resistance of a material's surface to mechanical penetration, abrasion, scratching, and indentation.
- **Toughness:** Ability of a material to absorb kinetic impact energy without catastrophic brittle fracture (total area under stress-strain curve).
- **Ductility:** Capacity of a material to undergo extensive plastic deformation in tension (drawn into wire) without rupture.
- **Malleability:** Capacity of a material to deform plastically under compressive force (hammering, rolling into sheets) without cracking.
- **Stiffness:** Ability to resist elastic deflection under applied load, measured by Young's Modulus ( = \\sigma / \\epsilon$).

## Standardised Mechanical Testing

- **Tensile testing (Tensometer):** Pulling a standard dogbone test piece at constant strain rate to record force-extension data and calculate $\\sigma$, $\\epsilon$, $, yield point, and UTS.
- **Hardness testing methods:** Rockwell (measures depth of indentation under major load using diamond cone), Vickers (diamond pyramid leaving square indent measured microscopically), Brinell (hardened steel ball indent).
- **Impact testing (Charpy & Izod):** Standard notched specimen struck by a swinging pendulum; potential energy absorbed during fracture measured in Joules on calibrated scale.
