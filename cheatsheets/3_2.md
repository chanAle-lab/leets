# Topic 3.2 · Design for Manufacture (DFM) & Sustainability
**Specification Strand:** 3. Implications of Wider Issues
**Syllabus Focus:** DFM principles, part count reduction, modularity, repairability, Ethical Trading Initiative (ETI), eco-materials, upcycling

---

## Design for Manufacture and Assembly (DFMA)

- **Part count minimisation:** Integrating multiple functional features (e.g. snap fits, bearing mounts, wire clips) into unified injection mouldings or die castings.
- **Poka-Yoke (Mistake proofing):** Asymmetric locator pins, keyed plugs, and polarized connectors making incorrect manual assembly physically impossible.
- **Top-down unidirectional assembly:** Designing parts to stack vertically in the Z-axis, eliminating tool reorientation and accelerating robotic assembly.
- **Standardisation:** Restricting hardware to single screw sizes (e.g. M3 Torx throughout), eliminating tool changes during workshop and factory assembly.
- **Design for Disassembly (DFD):** Using threaded or snap fasteners rather than irreversible chemical adhesives or sonic welds to facilitate maintenance and component recycling.

## Ethical Trade & Sustainable Materials

- **Ethical Trading Initiative (ETI):** Global code requiring fair living wages, freely chosen employment, safe working conditions, reasonable hours, and zero child labour in supply chains.
- **Eco-materials:** Certified sustainable timber (FSC/PEFC), bio-based resins, recycled rPET/rHDPE polymers, and solvent-free waterborne powder coatings.
- **Upcycling:** Repurposing manufacturing scrap or discarded products into higher-value engineered products without energy-intensive melting or re-refining.
